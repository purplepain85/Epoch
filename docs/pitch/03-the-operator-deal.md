## What you get

**The pipeline.** A production system that takes source news and turns it
into ready-to-publish content for Reddit, Facebook, X, and TikTok. You do
not write the articles. You do not format the graphics. You do not run the
templates. The hub does that.

**The voice controls.** Every market has a spin filter — tone, angle,
emphasis. You set it once. The pipeline produces to your spec. Adjust it
whenever you want.

**The comment layer.** Every article gets an automatic comment thread.
Every podcast episode gets a pre-cast and a post-cast thread. Every fan
who cares enough can post, re-share with an endorsement, or write a
downloadable rant that belongs to them.

**The podcast integrations.** You add the team-friendly podcasts in your
city to your market. Their episodes sync in automatically. Their fanbases
become part of your market's audience.

**The dashboard.** A real-time view of your market — how many articles
published, how many comments, how much revenue, which podcasts are
performing, which stories drove the most engagement. Everything you need
to know how it's working.

**The revenue layer.** Affiliate links, ad revenue, and podcast shares all
route to your accounts. You keep the recurring money. Gridiron Central
takes a year-end cut based on your market's performance.

**The brand rules.** Applied automatically at publish time. You never have
to worry about trademark issues, attribution format, or team-name
constraints. The hub handles it.

## What you do

**You bring the audience.** This is the one thing the pipeline cannot
produce. You already have the group. You already have the followers. You
already have the relationships. You bring them into the market.

**You run the community.** Reply to comments. Moderate threads. Keep the
tone civil. The pipeline produces content. You produce the community.

**You find the podcasts.** You know the local shows. You know which ones
are team-friendly and which ones are not. You reach out. You negotiate
the terms within the standard. You add them to your market.

**You make the local calls.** Which stories run. Which ones skip. Which
ones need a different angle. The pipeline offers defaults — you override
when it matters.

**You show up.** Football is 18 weeks plus playoffs. The market goes hot
during the season and cools during the off-season. You stay active all
year, but the season is where you earn.

## What you keep

- Every recurring dollar from affiliate revenue, ad revenue, and podcast
  shares goes to your accounts.
- Every relationship with your audience belongs to you.
- Every podcast partnership is yours to run and renegotiate.
- Every account you post from is yours.
- The audience list, the email list, the Discord — yours.

## What Gridiron Central takes

A single year-end cut based on your market's performance. That's it. No
upfront fee. No subscription. No monthly skim.

The cut is paid once a year, after the season, based on what the market
actually earned. If the market earned nothing, the cut is nothing. If the
market earned a lot, the cut is a percentage of the performance.

The number itself is negotiated per market at onboarding. The structure is
the same for everyone.

## What happens if it doesn't work

If your market underperforms — a slow year, a team that collapsed, an
audience that didn't materialize — Gridiron Central may pay back a portion
of the year-end cut as a help-back. This is discretionary and case by
case. The mechanism exists so that a bad year doesn't end the partnership.

## What happens if you want out

You can walk away at any time. The market position returns to the hub. The
audience, the accounts, and the podcast relationships stay with you as
the person who ran them. What does not stay with you is the pipeline. The
pipeline belongs to the hub, and only the hub.

If you want to keep the pipeline running after you leave, you buy that out
at a negotiated price. The buyout price is set at onboarding and covers
the pipeline, the templates, and the local market's event history.

## What happens if you misbehave

Violating the brand rules, causing trademark issues, running the market in
a way that damages the network — those are grounds for the position to be
revoked. If revoked, the year-end cut is settled at that point and the
position returns to the hub for re-listing.

The audience stays with the market. The accounts stay with the market. The
new operator picks up where you left off.

# The Revenue Model

## Overview

Three revenue streams exist in every market. The operator earns from all
three. The hub is paid through a service fee on the affiliate revenue that
routes through its system, plus a small monthly license.

That's the whole model. No cut of the operator's gross revenue. No year-end
percentage of their business. The hub is paid for the tool it provides and
for the traffic it routes.

## Stream 1 — Affiliate (operator's primary income)

The operator's affiliate links appear in articles, comment threads, and
podcast cards. When a reader clicks and converts, the operator earns.

Common programs in football content:

- Sportsbook signups (state-dependent, legal only where allowed)
- Ticket marketplaces (StubHub, SeatGeek, Vivid)
- Team merchandise (Fanatics, NFL Shop)
- Streaming (YouTube TV, NFL+, Paramount+)
- Fantasy platforms (DraftKings, FanDuel, Underdog, Sleeper)
- Sports info services (Action Network, BettingPros)

Standard payout per conversion:

- Sportsbook signup: $50–$200 depending on state and promotion
- Ticket purchase: 3–8% of order value
- Merchandise: 5–10% of order value
- Streaming signup: $10–$40
- Fantasy signup: $20–$80 per funded account

## Stream 2 — Ads (operator keeps all of it)

Display ads on market pages and within comment threads. Standard ad
networks (AdSense, Mediavine, Raptive) handle serving.

Typical RPM for sports content:

- In-season: $8–$25 per thousand impressions
- Off-season: $2–$6
- Comment thread ads: $3–$10

The operator keeps 100% of ad revenue. The hub does not take a cut here.

## Stream 3 — Podcast shares (negotiated per podcast)

Team-friendly podcasts integrated into the market share a portion of the
activity they generate. Podcast keeps most, operator takes a slice,
negotiated per episode or per month.

The hub does not participate in podcast revenue. It facilitates the
integration and gets paid through the affiliate layer.

## How the hub gets paid

Two sources. Both modest. Both real.

### License fee

A small monthly platform fee per market. Covers hosting, the pipeline,
the event log, and support. This is the floor — it keeps the hub running
whether or not any given market does well.

Range: **$100–$300/month per market**, negotiated at onboarding.

### Affiliate routing fee

The hub operates the affiliate redirector. Every affiliate link in a
market routes through it. When a conversion happens, the affiliate
network pays the hub, and the hub passes on the operator's share minus
a routing fee.

Range: **5–15% of affiliate revenue routed through the hub.**

The operator keeps the rest. Every month. All year. On every affiliate
conversion their market produces through the hub's infrastructure.

## What the hub does not take

- No cut of ad revenue
- No cut of podcast revenue
- No cut of sponsored content
- No year-end cut on the operator's total business
- No upfront fee
- No equity stake

The hub's income comes from the license fee and the routing fee. That's
it. Everything else is the operator's.

## Why this is enforceable

The operator keeps their accounts, their audience, and their relationships.
The hub owns the tracking redirector and the affiliate network accounts.
The routing fee is paid at the point of conversion — the affiliate network
pays the hub because the tracking ID belongs to the hub. The hub then
distributes the operator's share.

There is no claim on revenue the operator earns independently. If they
run a direct affiliate deal with a merchant, that's theirs. The routing
fee only applies to revenue that flows through the hub's redirector.

## Compliance notes

Sports betting affiliate marketing is regulated. Depending on the state
and the operator's activities, licensing may be required. This is not
optional. The hub will maintain a compliance framework covering:

- FTC disclosure requirements for all affiliate links
- State-level affiliate licensing where required
- Age gating where applicable
- Geographic restrictions on sportsbook promotions

The operator is responsible for compliance in their local jurisdiction.
The hub provides the tools and templates but not the legal advice.

## Realistic numbers — one mid-sized market, mid-season

| Stream | Monthly | Hub share |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Affiliate | $1,500–$6,000 | 5–15% of routed |
| Ads | $2,000 | 0% |
| Podcast shares | $300–$1,500 | 0% |
| **Total operator income** | **$3,800–$9,500/month** | — |
| **Hub income per market** | **$150–$900/month + license** | — |

Annualized per market:

- **Operator:** $45,000–$115,000/year
- **Hub:** $2,000–$5,000/year in routing fees, plus license

Across 32 markets:

- **Hub at 32 markets, mid-range:** $100,000–$200,000/year

The scale is what makes it work. One market is not a business. Thirty-two
markets, each paying a license and routing affiliate traffic through the
hub, is a real operation.

## The pitch in one sentence

You keep everything you earn. The hub takes a small license fee and a
small routing fee on affiliate traffic it processes for you. If the market
doesn't earn, the hub still runs — because that's what the license covers.

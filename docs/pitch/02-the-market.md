## Why football

The NFL is the biggest sports property in North America. More viewers per
game than any other league. More betting volume. More fantasy leagues.
More podcasts. More fan groups. More arguments at the dinner table about
a coach's decision on fourth down.

It is also the most consistent. Every fall, the fanbases refill. Every
Sunday, millions of people sit down to watch. Every week, there are new
stories to talk about.

Football fans are also the most engaged sports fans in the country. They
don't just watch. They read, they argue, they post, they buy merch, they
travel, they join groups, they run group chats, they run pages, they run
subreddits. The passion is not a seasonal thing. It is a year-round
identity.

That's the audience. That's why this works.

## Why 32

There are 32 teams in the NFL. Every one of them has a fanbase. Every
fanbase already has someone running its conversations.

Right now, those people are doing it alone. Facebook group admins,
subreddit moderators, X account runners, Discord server owners. They do
the work, they eat the drama, they keep the peace — and they pay for it
out of their own pocket, if they pay at all.

Gridiron Central changes that. One market per team. One operator per
market. Thirty-two chances for the person already doing the work to get
paid for it.

## Why now

Three things have come together in the last two years:

1. **AI can rehash news convincingly.** The pipeline can take a source
   article and produce a rewritten version in a market's voice in
   seconds. This did not exist in 2022.

2. **Sports betting and content apps have made affiliate revenue serious
   money.** The CPMs and conversion rates in sports content are the
   highest they have ever been.

3. **Podcast networks have saturated and podcasts are looking for
   distribution.** Hundreds of team-focused podcasts exist and every one
   of them wants more listeners. They will partner with whoever can give
   them reach.

None of those three conditions existed together before 2024. They do now.

## Why football and not another sport

Other sports have fans. Only football has this scale of weekly
engagement, this many podcasts per team, this level of betting interest,
and this cultural weight in so many cities at once.

Basketball, baseball, hockey — all worth doing eventually. None of them
have the same density of attention concentrated into the same 18 weeks
of the year.

Football is the market. Everything else is a follow-on.

## What the market is not

It is not a fan page. It is not a blog. It is not a podcast.

Those things already exist — thousands of them, in every city. They are
not the market.

The market is the place where the news meets the conversation. Where a
story drops and 300 fans respond within the hour. Where a podcast episode
sparks a thread that runs all week. Where the rant of a frustrated fan
after a bad loss gets shared into another fanbase and comes back as a
joke that lasts a month.

The market is the place where the fanbase lives.

## Where the money is

- **Affiliate.** Sportsbook signups, ticket links, merch links, streaming
  links, fantasy platforms. The operator earns per conversion. The
  industry standard in sports is $20–$200 per conversion on the major
  sportsbook offers.
- **Ads.** Direct display ads on the market's pages and within comment
  threads. Modest per impression, but with high volume, meaningful.
- **Podcast shares.** Every team-friendly podcast integrated into the
  market pays the operator a share of the activity it generates.
- **Sponsored content.** Occasionally, a local advertiser pays for a
  sponsored post or segment. Standard rates apply.

None of those are new. What's new is that they're all happening at one
place, for one market, run by one operator, at scale across 32 markets.

## The window

The window for this is open now. It will not be open forever.

The first mover in any market category captures the best positions. The
32 operators who move first will own their teams' markets outright. Anyone
who tries to build the same thing in 2027 will be competing against an
established network with traffic history, audience relationships, and
podcast partnerships already in place.

The market is the 32. The window is now.

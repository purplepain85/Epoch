## Who the operator is

One person per market. They run one team's market, in one city, for one
audience. They are the local face of Gridiron Central in their market.

They are not employees. They are not contractors. They are not
franchisees. They are independent operators who license the platform
and run their own business under it.

## What the operator is responsible for

- Bringing the audience (existing community, existing followers)
- Running the community (moderation, replies, tone)
- Finding and qualifying team-friendly podcasts
- Making local editorial calls
- Paying the monthly license fee
- Following the brand rules
- Reporting accurate revenue numbers

## What the operator is not responsible for

- Producing content (the pipeline produces it)
- Formatting (the pipeline formats it)
- Tracking affiliate conversions (the hub tracks them)
- Rules compliance (the rules engine enforces it)
- Hosting or infrastructure (the hub hosts it)

## Onboarding

### Step 1 — Screening

Before onboarding, the operator is screened on:

- Existing audience size (minimum threshold)
- Duration of activity (at least one full season)
- Track record of engagement
- Reputation in their team's fanbase
- Willingness to follow the brand rules

### Step 2 — Agreement

The operator signs an operator agreement. It defines:

- The market they operate
- The license fee and billing terms
- The affiliate routing fee
- The buyout prices for each clause
- The revocation terms
- The help-back criteria
- The exit terms

### Step 3 — Configuration

The operator configures their market:

- Team colors
- Voice (spin filter settings)
- Output channels (accounts for Reddit, FB, X, TikTok)
- Podcast list (RSS feeds and terms)
- Cadence (posts per day per platform)
- Moderation rules

Configuration takes about a day. Most of it is filling in a form.

### Step 4 — First week

During the first week:

- The operator reviews content before it publishes
- They tune the spin filter based on what they see
- They announce the market to their existing audience
- The first comment threads open
- They learn the dashboard

By the end of week one, the market is live.

### Step 5 — First month

During the first month:

- The operator grows engagement
- The first affiliate revenue lands
- The first podcast integrates
- The first sponsored content if timing is right
- The operator sees the first monthly summary

By month two, the market is self-sustaining.

## The dashboard

The operator's dashboard shows:

### Today

- Articles published today (count, by platform)
- Comments today
- Re-shares today
- Rants today
- Engagement score for the day
- Top performing piece

### This week

- Weekly total engagement
- Revenue so far this week
- Podcast activity
- Moderation actions
- Queue of pending content

### This month

- Monthly total engagement
- Monthly revenue breakdown
- Affiliate conversions
- Ad revenue (if self-reported)
- Podcast shares
- Trend vs. last month

### This year

- Year-to-date engagement
- Year-to-date revenue
- Year-end projection
- Chain verification status
- License fee and routing fees paid
- Help-back eligibility status

### Controls

- Spin filter adjustment
- Cadence adjustment
- Podcast management (add, remove, pause)
- Moderation tools
- Queue approval
- Voice overrides per article

## Moderation tools

- Comment review queue
- Flagged comments
- User management (ban, restrict, unban)
- Keyword filters
- Automated slur detection
- Report log

Every moderation action is logged. Nothing is silent.

## Operator responsibilities over time

### Daily

- Check the dashboard
- Review flagged comments
- Reply to comments where it matters
- Confirm the day's queue (in manual mode)
- Handle any alerts

### Weekly

- Review the week's top pieces
- Adjust the spin filter if needed
- Add or remove podcasts
- Respond to any affiliate network questions

### Monthly

- Review the monthly summary
- Pay the license fee
- Report ad revenue (if self-reported)
- Plan the next month's cadence

### Yearly

- Review the year-end summary
- Verify the chain seal
- Decide on the next year's configuration
- Negotiate help-back if applicable
- Consider buyout or exit if desired

## The operator's voice

The operator's personal identity and the market's voice are separate.

- The market voice is defined by the spin filter. It's the voice of
  the content.
- The operator's personal identity is theirs. They run the market,
  but the market is not "them."

This separation is intentional. If a market transfers to a new
operator, the market voice can stay consistent while the person
changes.

## Multi-market operators

An operator can run more than one market if:

- They have audiences for both
- They can maintain quality on both
- They pay the license fee for both

Multi-market operators have access to a combined dashboard showing
metrics across their markets.

## Leaving the market

The operator can exit at any time.

When they exit:

- The position returns to the hub
- The audience returns to the market (not the operator)
- The operator keeps their accounts
- The operator keeps their podcast relationships
- The operator keeps any sponsored content relationships
- The pipeline and event log stay with the market

If the operator wants to keep any of the hub's tools after exit, they
use a buyout clause. Prices are set at onboarding.

## Revocation

The hub can revoke a market position if the operator:

- Violates the brand rules in a way that creates trademark or legal
  risk
- Fails to pay the license fee for 60+ days
- Fails to moderate their community in a way that damages the network
- Fails to disclose accurate revenue information
- Abandons the market for a full season without notice

Revocation is:

- Documented with specific reasons
- Logged in the event log
- Followed by a settlement based on the current state
- Followed by a re-listing of the market

The operator is not banned from the platform. They can be considered
for other markets or reapply after a fixed period.

## Transfer of market position

A market position can be transferred to a new operator:

- By the current operator (with hub approval)
- By the hub (if the current operator exits)
- By mutual agreement

The new operator inherits:

- The market configuration
- The event log
- The affiliate routing relationship
- The podcast list

The new operator does not inherit:

- The prior operator's accounts
- The prior operator's personal audience
- The prior operator's reputation

The transfer is priced based on the market's state. A market with a
well-developed audience and history is worth more than a new one. The
buyout prices are set at the time of transfer.

## What makes a good operator

- Present. They show up daily during the season, weekly in the
  off-season.
- Active. They comment, reply, and moderate. They are not passive.
- Consistent. Their voice stays steady. Their market stays civil.
- Local. They know the team, the city, the culture, the podcasts.
- Honest. They report their numbers accurately.
- Patient. They don't expect overnight results. They build over a
  season.

## What makes a bad operator

- Absent. They set it up and walk away.
- Passive. They let the comments run wild.
- Volatile. They change the voice every week.
- Generic. Their market doesn't feel local.
- Sloppy. They miss deadlines, break rules, and create issues.
- Impatient. They leave after two months because revenue didn't
  materialize fast enough.

## Screening for the right ones

The screening criteria exist to catch the second list before they take
a market. It's easier to reject a bad operator than to revoke one
after they've built an audience.

But screening also recognizes that no one is perfect. An operator with
a strong audience but a weak podcast network can grow into the role. An
operator with good instincts but a small audience might still be worth
trying in a smaller market.

The right person is the one who will show up, week after week, and
grow the market steadily over a full season. Everything else is
trainable.
